## Shared creative direction

**Campaign thought: Your hospital. Your workflow.**

Tone: calm, precise and commercially credible. Buyers should see software solving recognizable work, not a futuristic hospital fantasy. Suggested balance: 75% real verified interface footage, 15% editorial typography/diagrams, 10% licensed or clearly illustrative hospital atmosphere. Avoid fabricated testimonials, fake metrics, AI doctor endorsements, robots, holograms and generic stock montages.

Suggested visual system, subject to brand approval: deep navy #102631, warm white #F5F4EF, restrained teal #197B78; one licensed sans-serif family, generous spacing and high contrast. Match product colors where appropriate rather than recoloring the application deceptively. Use match cuts, clean masks and controlled 105–115% pushes. No spinning dashboards or unreadable full-screen tables.

Voice: warm, authoritative Indian English, approximately 125–140 words/minute. Human or licensed AI voice with pronunciation review; never clone a real person's voice without consent. English captions; create a separately timed Hindi/regional-language version only after copy approval. Do not simply speed up a translated script.

Master deliverables per video: 1080x1920 vertical and separately reframed 1920x1080 landscape, 30 fps, H.264 MP4 with AAC 48 kHz, clean master, captioned master, SRT, thumbnail and editable source project. Aim around -14 LUFS integrated / no more than -1 dBTP; audition on a phone, laptop and headphones. Duck licensed music beneath narration. Keep important text away from platform UI overlays; verify actual destination-platform safe areas before export.

Capture clean 1440p/4K UI footage where available, hide notifications, standardize browser zoom, rehearse cursor actions, and record handles before/after each action. Reframe details for vertical instead of shrinking a desktop screen. Use synthetic records labelled DEMO. No real addresses, patient IDs, phone numbers, medical documents, revenue or credential fields. Review every frame; blurring a name alone is insufficient.

## Editing workflow and acceptance criteria

1. Lock brand, primary buyer, language, CTA and evidence-approved script. Read scripts aloud; adjust narration before timing shots.
2. Capture verified synthetic-data flows with a shot log linking each take to the acceptance test. Save screen clean plates and room tone/music handles where relevant.
3. Build a rough cut with readable UI and actual narration. Show one action per shot; remove cursor wandering and long loading pauses without implying measured speed.
4. Add disciplined motion graphics, UI focus boxes and subtitles. No more than two caption lines at once; caption meaning in coherent phrases. Make all critical narrative understandable muted.
5. Review product accuracy with a domain owner, visual craft with a fresh viewer, and buyer relevance with a hospital administrator if available.
6. Mix narration/music and export separately framed vertical and landscape versions. Inspect phone-scale text, subtitle timing, clipping, black frames, first/last frames and endpoint durations.
7. Run privacy/rights/claims approval. Test clickable destination, lead submission and attribution before publishing. QR is optional on landscape presentations, not the only mobile CTA.

Release checklist: no placeholders; no actual patient data; no unapproved hospital logo; no fabricated feature or benefit metric; no security/compliance claims without proof; no unlicensed music/voice/font/footage; no UI too small to read; CTA held for at least five seconds; captions correct and inside safe area; lead routing tested; product workflow approved.
