# Custom hospital software: qualified-pipeline plan

Status: proposed strategy, not a launched campaign. Based on the inspected project, with live workflow proof still pending. Planning assumption: independent Indian hospitals; validate geography, language, implementation capacity and budget before spending. "Shree Kamal" is the current project/hospital identity, NOT automatically the commercial software brand or a customer endorsement.

## 1. Positioning

**Hospital software shaped around the way your hospital works.**

Supporting line: "Bring clinical, billing and pharmacy workflows into a hospital-specific implementation, with a clearly scoped rollout."

Do not lead with AI, technology stack, a huge module count or "cheapest ERP." Buyers need confidence that their staff can operate it, finances reconcile and implementation will not interrupt care. Use AI for production assistance, not as an invented clinical product feature.

Differentiate with a concrete process: workflow discovery -> mapped demonstration -> fixed-scope pilot -> staff acceptance -> phased rollout. These are proposed services to offer only after confirming capacity, not an existing service guarantee.

## 2. Initial customer profile

Start with an assumed 20–100-bed independent multispecialty hospital in a geography you can support. This is a test segment, not established product capacity. Prioritize hospitals with disconnected billing/pharmacy tools, manual IPD charge reconciliation or a replacement project already under consideration.

| Buyer | Concern | Proof to show | Discovery question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner/director | Operational visibility and implementation risk | Scoped pilot and a reconciled patient journey | Which workflow makes management intervene most often? |
| Administrator | Staff handoffs and usability | Reception -> department workflow | Where is information re-entered today? |
| Finance head | Charge completeness and reconciliation | Itemized IPD bill, advances and balances | Which charges are reconciled outside your current system? |
| Medical superintendent | Clinical continuity and adoption | Consultation, prescription and result visibility | What must a doctor see in a single encounter? |
| IT/implementation lead | Access, migration, recovery and integration | Written architecture/security evidence and test outcomes | Which integrations and retention requirements are mandatory? |

Deprioritize buyers seeking certified capabilities, chain-wide tenancy, interoperability or guaranteed uptime that you cannot yet demonstrate. Do not promise ABDM, NABH, HIPAA, insurance APIs or medical-device integrations without evidence and applicable review.

## 3. Offer and sales process

Primary CTA across all five videos: **Book a 20-minute hospital workflow review.**

The review covers one priority workflow; it is not an open-ended free implementation consultation. Deliver a one-page fit/gap summary and propose a focused demonstration. Ask for no patient data.

Commercial structure to validate:
- Discovery: workflow map, requirements and acceptance criteria.
- Pilot: bounded departments, synthetic/UAT data, agreed users and success criteria.
- Implementation: approved customization, migration, training and deployment milestones.
- Ongoing support: hosting if applicable, maintenance, support hours and escalation terms.
- Separate custom integrations and change requests from the base scope. Quote from delivery effort and margin, not an invented per-bed price.

Sales stages: qualified conversation -> workflow review held -> tailored demo -> agreed scope/budget -> pilot proposal -> paid pilot -> rollout acceptance. Do not mark a form submission as a sales-qualified opportunity.

## 4. Conversion destination

Build a software-vendor landing page separate from the hospital's patient-facing appointment website. Proposed structure:
1. Positioning headline, 30-second overview video and workflow-review CTA.
2. Three workflow outcomes supported by actual demo clips.
3. Configuration versus bespoke development: clear inclusions and limits.
4. Pilot/migration/training process with explicit dependencies.
5. Evidence: only approved references, measured results and verifiable security statements.
6. Fit/gap FAQ, contact details and short qualification form.

Form fields: name, business contact, hospital name/city, role, bed-range, current system, priority problem, intended timeline, preferred callback time and consent to contact. Make detailed budget discussion optional until discovery. No medical documents or patient details. Test success screen, notifications, duplicate handling and mobile usability.

Qualify on hospital fit, decision-making involvement, concrete problem, timeline and commercial feasibility. A useful initial rubric is five dimensions scored 0–2; 7+ triggers prioritized review, 4–6 nurture, 0–3 reassess. Calibrate from actual sales, not vanity scores.

## 5. Distribution: account-led first, paid expansion second

- Build a focused list of 50 hospital accounts from public business sources or referrals. Record organization/role/reason for fit, not scraped personal health data.
- Publish founder-led LinkedIn posts with one video and one operational question. Connect with relevant decision-makers thoughtfully; do not spam.
- Seek introductions from hospital consultants and existing professional contacts. Disclose referral arrangements where required.
- Use email or consent-based WhatsApp follow-up appropriate to local rules and platform policy. Never use the hospital's patient database as a software-marketing audience.
- Test paid LinkedIn/Meta only after tracking, qualification and demo readiness. Platform targeting is imperfect; hospital/role fields do the final qualification.
- Use retargeting only with proper consent/privacy setup and enough audience size. If volume is too low, use relevant follow-up content rather than forcing a tiny ad audience.

Sample initial outreach:
> We are developing hospital-specific software implementations around OPD, IPD billing and pharmacy workflows. Is charge reconciliation or interdepartmental handoff a current priority at your hospital? If useful, I can share a short demonstration and arrange a 20-minute workflow review. We would not need patient data.

Follow-up after an expressed interest: share the one relevant departmental video, propose two meeting slots, and ask which workflow they want demonstrated. After the review, send a fit/gap summary and the agreed next step. Respect opt-outs and avoid repeated unsolicited messaging.

## 6. Four-week launch sequence

| Phase | Work | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|
| Readiness, before launch | Resolve audit blockers, verify synthetic workflows, confirm brand/CTA and film proof | Safe and repeatable demo; truthful scripts approved |
| Week 1 | Launch landing page/tracking; share video 1; contact first 15 qualified accounts | Lead receipt works; first buyer objections collected |
| Week 2 | Share videos 2 and 3 by buyer role; contact next 20 accounts; hold workflow reviews | Learn which operational problem earns meetings |
| Week 3 | Share video 4; run a small approved paid test on best messages | Compare qualified meetings, not raw clicks |
| Week 4 | Share video 5; follow up with warm accounts; tailored demos and scoped proposals | Decide continue, revise message or pause spending |

Schedule starts after readiness gates, not automatically on today's date. No campaigns, messages or automations have been created.

## 7. Measurement and budget discipline

Track source/medium/campaign and per-video `utm_content` values: `v01_custom_fit`, `v02_opd`, `v03_ipd_billing`, `v04_pharmacy_lab`, `v05_pilot`.

Funnel: landing visit -> submitted inquiry -> qualified inquiry -> review booked -> review held -> accepted opportunity -> paid pilot -> rollout. Deduplicate organizations and track offline meeting outcomes in your CRM.

Primary measures: cost per held qualified review, opportunity rate, pilot conversion, sales cycle and contribution margin. Secondary: three-second hold, completion rate, click-through and form conversion. High views with no hospital decision-makers is not success.

Illustrative media experiment only: INR 30,000 excluding production, taxes and sales labor. Allocate INR 12,000 each to two competing messages and INR 6,000 to the stronger qualified-meeting performer or suitable retargeting; do not fragment into five tiny cold campaigns. Obtain approval before spend. Start smaller/account-led if budget or audience is insufficient.

Economics: allowable acquisition spend per customer = expected first-year gross profit x approved acquisition fraction. Allowable cost per held qualified review = allowable customer acquisition spend x observed review-to-customer close rate. Until real rates exist, these are hypotheses, not forecasts. Never guarantee leads or revenue.

Diagnostic rules: good views/poor clicks -> revise CTA and relevance; clicks/poor fit -> revise targeting and qualification; qualified meetings/no pilot -> investigate scope, proof, trust or price; no reliable tracking -> stop spend until fixed.

## 8. Pre-publication approvals

Confirm seller/company name, logo usage rights, target region/language, domain/contact, implementation/support capacity, budget and offer. Obtain written permission before naming a hospital as a customer or showing its branding. Use only synthetic records. Film only workflows that pass the companion audit acceptance matrix.
